# Pilot Talk Track — Genesis Gold Group

> Verbal-channel playbook for pilot #1. The written report carries the numbers;
> this doc carries the conversation. Presentation order and every number are
> locked in `METHODOLOGY.md` — this doc never invents a number the report
> can't defend.

Last updated: **2026-06-11**

---

## Who's at the table

Genesis Gold Group — precious-metals dealer (Gold/Silver IRAs, 401k rollovers),
faith-branded, customers 59+. ~19 employees, Beverly Hills + Las Vegas.
Likely room: **Brad Garrett (co-founder)**, possibly COO/CFO. No RevOps function —
nobody owns data hygiene today.

What their world looks like:
- Client Outreach Specialists making **200+ outbound calls/day**
- AE closers at **$150–250K OTE**; a closed deal is worth **$5–15K in commission**
- Database fed by **purchased premium leads** plus inbound
- Not HubSpot; CRM unknown — confirm on the call
- Financial services: compliance-aware, reputation-sensitive

**Translation rules for this room.** This is a phone-first lead-buying shop, not a
SaaS email org:
- "Unreachable contact" → **"a lead that was never going to pick up or reply"**
- CPRC → **cost per reachable lead** (their pricing is per-lead; the math is identical)
- "Renewal" → **next month's lead order.** No renewal date needed — they can
  reallocate spend immediately. Right-sizing is instant here.
- Lever 1 is **paid dialing hours**, not just email sends. 200 dials/day against a
  list where a third is dead = a third of payroll spent on ghosts.

## Before the call (non-negotiable prep)

1. **Get the file early.** CSV export with: email, phone, lead source, and any
   last-contact / last-disposition timestamps. Offer to walk their admin through
   the export — unknown CRM, assume nothing.
2. **Run the audit before the meeting.** This is a founder-delivered audit, not a
   live demo. Walk in with the report finished. Nothing is computed in front of
   the prospect.
3. **Fact-finder asks** (phone or email, before the meeting — every blank gets a
   flagged default, never a silent one):
   - How many outreach specialists? How many AEs/closers?
   - Loaded comp for an outreach specialist (or salary — we can load it)
   - Lead spend: which sources, what per lead or per month?
   - Rough connect/reply rate, and lead → deal rate
   - Average revenue per closed deal
   - What CRM, and who manages it
4. **Spend map.** Per-vendor spend is what powers the scorecard. If they'll only
   share total lead spend, the scorecard still prices what it can — flag the rest.

## Run of show (30 minutes)

### 1. Frame — the empty side of the table *(2 min)*

The opener is the asymmetry, not the product:

> "Every lead vendor you buy from grades their own homework. They tell you the
> leads are good, they hold all the data, and nobody on your side of the table
> has an independent number. I'm the buyer's side of that table. I measured
> your file — here's what you actually bought."

Origin story lands here if it fits — verbal channel only, insight not grievance:
you watched a sales org spin on bad data while every dashboard said the team was
hitting activity targets. The damage was invisible because nobody measured
reachability. *(Never appears in the written report.)*

### 2. The finding — one number *(2 min)*

> "X% of the contacts in your database cannot be reached. Not 'low quality' —
> unreachable: disconnected, departed, abandoned, or noise that accepts contact
> and never responds."

Say the locked definition out loud, once, early. Every later number inherits it.

### 3. Lever 1 — paid hours spent on ghosts *(5 min — the headline)*

> "Your outreach team makes 200-plus calls a day. At X% unreachable, roughly N
> of those dials every single day were never going to connect, no matter how
> good the rep is. Across the team that's $___ a year of payroll spent calling
> ghosts."

Formula per methodology: reps × loaded comp × selling-time% (35% default,
flagged) × unreachable%. This is the line that stops the room — it hits their
own payroll, and at their OTE levels it's big.

### 4. Source map — who sold you the dead leads *(5 min)*

> "Here's the part nobody can show you but me: which lead source the dead
> records came from. Source A is X% unreachable. Source B is Y%. Your inbound
> runs Z%."

This is the differentiator. No lead vendor will ever publish this table.

### 5. CPRC — cost per reachable lead *(5 min — the money slide)*

> "You pay $__ per premium lead. After removing the ones that can't be reached,
> your real cost is $__ per reachable lead from Source A, and $__ from Source B.
> Same budget, very different value. Here's what next month's order looks like
> if you only pay for leads that are real."

The right-sizing is immediate for them — no renewal date, just next month's
purchase order. This is the slide that pays for the audit on the spot.

**If they push back on "reachable":** that fight is pre-won — restate the locked
definition, point to the record-by-record evidence file. Never argue
projections; the file is their own data.

### 6. Lever 2 — the upside, framed as a choice *(3 min)*

> "Either you keep paying $___ for ghosts, or those same dialing hours go into
> leads that pick up — at your close rates that's $___ in recoverable revenue.
> Same hours, two ways to count them. Pick either number; don't add them."

Pre-empt the double-count objection before anyone senses it.

### 7. Deliverability — the fear *(2 min)*

> "If you emailed this list today, about X% would hard-bounce. Sender-reputation
> guidance says keep that under 2%, and sending platforms start suspending
> accounts around 5%. And this lands on your whole domain: your reps send from
> their own inboxes, so a bad list burns every rep's deliverability at once,
> including to the good leads."

If they ask whether their platform protects them: "No — sequences send from
your reps' own mailboxes, so the damage is yours. And on platform sends,
HubSpot protects itself by suspending you." (Defensibility note: Google's
published Feb 2024 ceiling is a 0.3% SPAM-COMPLAINT rate for domains sending
5,000+/day to Gmail. There is no published Google bounce threshold — don't
claim one.)

Financial-services bonus: their brand cannot afford to look like a spam shop to
a 65-year-old retiree's inbox provider.

### 8. Lever 3 + evidence file — one breath, then the asks *(2 min)*

> "And for completeness: X% of your lead spend bought unreachable records —
> that's $___ documented, record by record, in the evidence file you keep.
> Where any vendor agreement includes replacement or quality terms, that file
> supports the conversation."

That last sentence is the ONLY recapture mention in the meeting. Upside, never
the promise.

### 9. The asks *(4 min)*

1. **The audit engagement** — $2–5K one-time, report delivered, walkthrough
   included. (This meeting may BE the paid audit if the pilot is comped — then
   the ask is the quarterly.)
2. **Quarterly cadence** — $1–2K/quarter: "Lead quality isn't a one-time event.
   The vendors that scored well this quarter degrade. The quarterly re-audit is
   how you keep your per-reachable-lead cost honest." First recurring revenue.
3. **The case study** — anonymized is fine: "a precious-metals dealer with a
   200-dial-a-day outbound motion."
4. **One intro** — they know other shops that buy leads. One warm intro per
   happy pilot is the growth loop.

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "Our reps sell way more than 35% of their time." | "Even better — that makes the waste number bigger, not smaller. More selling hours against the same dead list means more payroll burned. 35% is the conservative floor from Salesforce and RAIN field studies; tell me your number and I'll rerun it live." |
| "Our lead vendor guarantees quality." | "They grade their own homework. This is the independent measurement — and your own file is the evidence. If their numbers and mine disagree, we can both look at the same records." |
| "How do you define reachable?" | Restate the locked definition. Offer the record-by-record file. Never improvise a second definition. |
| "We can clean this ourselves." | "You can — verification tools are cheap. What they can't tell you is which vendor sold you the dead records and what you're really paying per reachable lead. That's the part that changes your next purchase order." |
| "Is our customer data safe?" | "Records are processed in memory and never stored. We keep scan metadata — counts and rates — not your contacts. You keep the file." |
| "Why should we pay for bad news?" | "The audit pays for itself if next month's lead order is even 10% better allocated. And if your data comes back clean, that's an independent receipt that it's clean — also worth knowing." |
| "What's the catch on the credit thing?" | "No catch and no promise. Some vendor agreements have quality terms, some don't. The evidence file works either way — its main job is your next purchase decision." |

## Do not

- **Do not promise credits or refunds.** Recapture is one sentence, late, framed
  as upside. (Locked in `DIRECTION.md`: a refund business runs on a policy the
  adversary controls.)
- **Do not stack Lever 1 and Lever 2.** Choice, never a sum.
- **Do not demo the tool.** Deliver the report. The product is the finding,
  not the software.
- **Do not mention integrations.** CSV in, report out. CRM connectors are
  post-pilot.
- **Do not let the origin story into the written deliverable.** Verbal only.
- **Do not invent numbers.** Anything not in the fact-finder gets the
  methodology default, flagged as an estimate, out loud.
